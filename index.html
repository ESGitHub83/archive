Starless Night — working draft
Can I steal your silence / Can I still your fears / Set fire to your loneliness / Drain your pool of tears / Banish all your demons / Watch them — waiting for your star to rise Can I steal your silence / On this starless starless night Search the skies for signs of life / On this starless starless night / There's a silent silent space / Beneath the screaming of the world / Where you sing your song now Let's magic darkness into light / On this starless starless night
The repetition is the point — starless starless, silent silent. The second word doesn't intensify, it settles. The whole song is doing that: not more of the feeling but deeper into it. Still a working draft — there's a note at the bottom, something about where it comes from, not yet resolved.

The uncanny — a working definition
Not horror, which is the response to an external threat. Not the supernatural, which is simply the impossible. Something more specific: the wrongness that comes from the familiar going slightly off.
Freud's Das Unheimliche — the unhomely. The four stories all live here. The cat discussing shortbread is Mode 3 comic uncanny. The cat in the chair resolving the widower's grief is Mode 2 emotional uncanny. Both run simultaneously. The comedy is the door — the feeling comes through it.
