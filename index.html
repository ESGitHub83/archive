story note
Six Million Dollar Monkey — novel (~47,000 words)
Edinburgh. Cormack is a temp engineer for the council, playing bass in a band called Six Million Dollar Monkey alongside Johan the feral singer and Ivor the grumpy engineer who communicates mainly in disapproving silences. A hole appears in Hanover Street that goes deeper than the city. An underground chamber with a giant monkey head carved into the altar wall. A cult called the Foundation, led by the smooth and sinister Mr Easterly, who books the band to play at their Festival of the Seed and then leads their followers into the hole. Johan goes with them. The street collapses. A letter surfaces from the bottom of the shaft addressed to Cormack. Self-published 2012. The novel that was going to become Discovery on Substack.

story note
Faces in the Firth — Part One (novel)
North Kessock. A village on the Firth north of Inverness. Sandy Morrison dies on the same day his daughter is born — crushed by a Highland cow dropped from a helicopter while the Wonder Stuff play on his car radio. He becomes a ghost lying on the Firth bed to find peace. His eight-year-old stepdaughter Alex Anderson has prophetic dreams and can talk to dolphins, stones, and seagulls. Her mother Pauline has the sight but it goes silent the day Sandy disappears. Jack Parsons runs the Lighthouse Estate — wealthy guests pay to hunt human beings on the grounds. His helpers Keg and Aldo source victims from Inverness while arguing about leather trousers. Parsons conducts rituals seeking an elemental he cannot raise. Hayley Anderson drives to the cliffs in a white dress in a trance, remembering gold and jewels and the voice of a god. At the village meeting Old Malc's ewes have given birth to giant eyeball creatures. Someone jumps from the Kessock Bridge. Parsons watches the press conference, sees Alex on TV, feels the elemental. A man called Adam Potter hears voices, sees Masher Fish in rivers controlled by traffic lights, was in love with Isabell. Sandy's ghost watches cars on Highland roads from the shadows of sorrowful trees. Two parts written. Multiple complete scenes in existence.
November 2001. Morag is here. The Kessock Bridge. Alex collecting stones and listening to them. Sandy crushed by a cow while the Wonder Stuff play on his car radio.

fragment
White dress on a clifftop
The tickling trickle of blood running down the inside of her wrist snapped her out of her deep trance with a start. She'd been gripping the steering wheel so tightly her long manicured nails had dug into her hands. A single drop of blood dropped unnoticed onto the front of her silk, white dress. Images raced through her head, random and confused — bright and noisy place with people laughing and drinking / nothing, a blank wall of light and a low bass hum / the brilliant colours of gold and jewels / the face of an angel / the voice of a god.
Hayley Anderson driving to the cliffs. From Faces in the Firth.

fragment
You can sense me, can't you?
You can sense me, can't you? You're looking right at me but to your dull, unquestioning eyes I'm simply not there. Wake up to the things that your senses draw you towards. Uneasy, nagging, knawing on your bones, weighing down your mind — a tiny voice which tells you with certainty that there is something moving through you, across you and within you that you can't quite grasp, can't quite see, can't quite put your finger on. Still you're drawn towards it. I'm standing, bathed in the shadows of these sorrowful trees. I look out from the gloom, watching as you approach the bend in the single track road just below where I stand. Enough. I've seen enough. On you go into your night. I'll go on into mine.
Sandy's ghost speaking to the occupants of a passing car on a Highland road. From the Part 2 outline of Faces in the Firth.
